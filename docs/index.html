Shell Coiling Genes

Snail shells, clams, nautilus and ram's horns are all one tube growing around an axis. Raup (1966) showed that four numbers describe most of them. This project asks which parts of the genome set those numbers.

Overview

The question

Raup's four parameters are W, how fast the tube widens each turn; D, how far it sits from the coiling axis; T, how far each turn moves down the axis; and S, the shape of the opening. Change them and a nautilus becomes a periwinkle or a clam. If a few genes control a few parameters, shell diversity has a simple genetic basis. The best place to look is the rough periwinkle, Littorina saxatilis, whose Crab and Wave forms differ in shell shape and have been crossed and genotyped.

What we did

  1. Mapped W, D, T-related and S in 350 periwinkles from a Crab × Wave cross (Koch et al. 2021).
  2. Placed the mapped regions on the 2024 genome and listed their genes.
  3. Checked which genes are active in the mantle, the tissue that builds the shell.
  4. Built gene trees for two candidate families across 19 other molluscs, human and fly.
  5. Followed the candidates through larval development in a mussel, an apple snail, an oyster and a clam, and into single cells of a mussel-relative larva.
  6. Matched the LG6 candidates to their oyster counterparts, compared them with known shell genes, and checked them against proteins found in the shells of six molluscs.
  7. Tested the periwinkle regions again in 1,130 wild snails (Koch et al. 2022).
  8. Placed the species with genomes among 358 snail shells measured in Raup's terms (Noshita et al. 2012).

What is still open

  • Which genes inside the LG6 inversion matter. Recombination is suppressed there, so mapping alone cannot separate them. The shortlist is now the crok-like Ly6 gene, rhoGAP6 and diaphanous.
  • Whether the periwinkle diaphanous gene affects shell shape, as its pond-snail relative affects coiling direction.
  • Clams, nautilus and horns. Few of these species have annotated genomes and measured shells, too few for a comparison across species.

The sections below hold the data behind each step. Every result is from public data; the code is at github.com/micahnyc/shell-coiling-genes.

4 · The pond-snail coiling gene

LG6 holds the periwinkle version of Lsdia1

In the pond snail Lymnaea, losing the formin Lsdia1 flips the coil from right- to left-handed (Davison et al. 2016). Protein alignment shows the LG6 diaphanous gene is periwinkle's match for it.

Periwinkle proteinvs Lymnaea Lsdia1vs Lymnaea Lsdia2
diaphanous-2-like, LG6 QTL XP_070197016.165.7%69.2%

Lsdia1 and Lsdia2 are 87.9% identical to each other: a duplication in the Lymnaea lineage. The gene tree in section 8 confirms periwinkle has a single diaphanous gene, the LG6 one. The LG3 "diaphanous-1-like" gene is a misnamed fragment that falls outside the clade.

5 · Mantle expression

Which candidates are switched on in shell-building tissue

Gene activity (TPM) from NCBI's tissue panel: mantle ×2, foot ×2, hepatopancreas ×2, reproductive ×2, penis, head. Genes are ranked by how mantle-biased they are among 23,211 expressed genes. Known shell genes (carbonic anhydrases, tyrosinase, chitin synthase, nacrein) rank in the top 2%, so the ranking picks up shell genes.

  • Diaphanous-2-like (LG6) is active in the mantle, but about as much in foot and head. Lsdia1 acts in the early embryo, so adult tissue cannot test it.
  • The LG17 dispatched genes are close to silent in the mantle. This weakens them as shell-growth candidates, at least in adults.

The S peak (14.8 Mb) sits beside CD109-like and cytochrome P450 2U1-like, but an F2 cross cannot tell genes inside the block apart. The mantle also makes mucus and immune proteins, so CD109, murinoglobulin and lipoxygenase may not be shell genes.

  • The mollusc-specific lineage is in 12 of 14 species with an external shell and none of the 5 without one (octopuses, squid, Aplysia, sea slug).
  • Those 5 absences come from only 2–3 independent losses, and a missing gene can be an annotation gap, so this is a lead, not a result.
  • Periwinkle's copies are barely active in the adult mantle. A test would need the developing shell gland or juvenile mantle.
  • Diaphanous is single-copy in 13 of 19 molluscs, including periwinkle and both left-coiling snails. Copy number does not track coiling direction.

Single cells settle it toward cilia

Single-cell RNA-seq of a Dreissena larva (a freshwater mussel relative, 575 cells at the shell-field stage) shows which cells use the gene. Its most active copy of the mollusc-only lineage is in 40% of the cells with the strongest cilia signature, and in 0–3% of cells leaning toward shell field. Cell by cell it tracks cilia genes (ρ = 0.36) much more than shell-field genes (ρ = 0.15).

10 · LG6 candidates in other molluscs

Which LG6 genes behave like shell genes elsewhere?

Each mantle-specific gene from the LG6 region was matched to its counterpart in the oyster, and checked for whether the oyster uses it mostly in the mantle. Nine known shell genes from periwinkle went through the same test as controls, and their oyster counterparts rank at a median of the 94th percentile for mantle bias, so the test picks up shell genes.

  • The candidates as a group sit well below the shell-gene controls (Mann-Whitney p = 3×10⁻⁴), so being mantle-specific in periwinkle is not enough.
  • The crok-like gene belongs to a family of 4 in periwinkle. The oyster gene matches the family rather than this one copy, but the LG6 copy is the most mantle-specific of the four (88 vs 2 TPM).
  • A gene tree of the Ly6 family shows the LG6 copy arose by a duplication in periwinkle's snail group. Its exact counterpart is in the whelk-like Babylonia. Its sister copy on LG2 is active in most tissues (90 vs 70 TPM), while the LG6 copy is active almost only in the mantle. One copy appears to have taken on a mantle job after the duplication. The wider family also exists in octopus and squid, so it is not limited to shelled species.
  • Mussel development and single larval cells were also tested, but the shell-gene controls do not behave consistently there, so those results carry little weight.

Proteins found in actual shell

Six molluscs have published shell proteomes: lists of proteins extracted from the shell itself (a limpet, two slipper snails, an abalone, a pearl oyster and a land snail). We matched all of them to periwinkle genes.

  • None of the shell proteins matches the three shortlisted genes directly.
  • The limpet's shell contains two Ly6-family proteins. Their periwinkle counterparts are in a crok-like cluster on LG2, right next to the sister copy of our LG6 gene. So the LG6 gene is a duplicate from a gene cluster whose limpet version ends up in shell. That makes it more plausible as a shell gene, without proving it.
  • Neither inversion holds more shell-protein genes than expected (LG6: 5 against 7.3 expected, mostly proteins carried over from cells; LG17: 1 against 3.7).
  • Periwinkle versions of shell proteins are three times more common than average among its most mantle-specific genes, which supports using mantle activity as a guide.
  • Formins never appear in shell, as expected for a protein that works inside cells. If diaphanous affects coiling, it does so during development.
11 · Wild snails

Do the inversions predict shell shape in the wild?

1,130 snails from three Swedish shores where Crab and Wave snails meet (Koch et al. 2022), each with the same shape measures as the F2 cross and a genotype for 16 inversions. Every test adjusts for habitat, island, sex and shell size, since inversions and shell shape both change between boulder and rock habitats. Effects are per inversion copy, in standard deviations.

InversionF2 crossWild snailsVerdict
LG6S and size (just below threshold)size −0.54 (p 10⁻⁶⁹), coil expansion W +0.23 (p 10⁻¹¹), opening size +0.16, S −0.15 (p 7×10⁻⁴); size and W hold with all 16 inversions in one modelreplicates, adds W
LG17W and D, inside the LG17 inversionW −0.10 (p 0.015), D +0.11 (p 0.02); without the habitat adjustment W p 0.0015. S +0.22 (p 10⁻⁵) and size, which mostly vanish alongside the other inversionsconsistent, not confirmed
LG12opening size (suggestive)height expansion −0.30 (p 10⁻²³), W −0.22, opening size −0.19, all holding in the joint modelreplicates
  • The F2 LG17 signal sits inside the LG17 inversion, which spans 63.0–70.3 Mb on the 2024 genome (inversion bounds from Koch et al. 2021, placed with our markers). The coil-expansion region, 63.9–70.3 Mb, is entirely within it.
  • In the wild this inversion goes up and down with habitat, so adjusting for habitat removes much of its effect. The cross shuffles inversions independently of habitat, which is why it shows the effect clearly. The wild data are consistent with it but cannot confirm it on their own.
  • These tests are for whole inversions. Recombination is suppressed inside an inversion, so the wild data cannot narrow LG6 down to genes.
